## What failed and why

**IT model graphs have completeness 0.20–0.47 (all below the 0.5 threshold).**

| Condition | Completeness range | feat_sum | error_sum |
|-----------|-------------------|----------|-----------|
| With chat template | 0.09–0.34 | negative | dominant |
| Without chat template | 0.20–0.47 | small positive | dominant |

Root cause: **activation distribution mismatch.** The CLT was trained on activations from
the IT model processing long clinical trial documents (raw text). At inference, the model
processes short QA prompts (optionally chat-template-wrapped). The distributions are so
different that ~7500 features fire per layer vs ~21 during training.

For comparison, the PT model CLT (trained on same corpus format as inference) achieved
completeness 0.55–0.81 (Phase 4) and 0.76–0.90 (Phase 5 Track B).

## Decision needed

The PT model is the only path with validated completeness. Four options:

1. **Return to PT model** — Use existing Phase 5 Track B results (completeness 0.76–0.90).
   Finish feature labeling, notebook 03, push toward publication. No additional compute.

2. **Retrain IT CLT on chat-wrapped data** — Wrap corpus chunks in Gemma chat template
   before extraction, retrain CLT. ~$50, ~10hrs. Risk: short prompts may still be OOD
   relative to long wrapped documents.

3. **Retrain IT CLT on short prompts** — Extract activations from a large diverse set of
   short QA prompts matching inference format. Different data strategy. Risk: may not
   have enough diverse medical QA data.

4. **Use Gemma Scope 2's 1B CLT** — Pre-trained CLT for gemma-3-1b, no training cost.
   But: much smaller model, may not have medical knowledge. And pipeline would need
   adaptation for 1B architecture.

## Key learnings (saved to memory)

- **IT model CLT must match inference format.** Raw corpus activations are OOD for
  chat-template-wrapped inference prompts. PT models don't have this problem because
  raw text IS the inference format.

- **Don't remove hook_mlp_out detach.** The detach is mathematically correct for the
  paper's linearised decomposition. Removing it causes double-counting (tested, verified
  on Pythia-70m, reverted).

- **The circuit tracing paper used an IT model** (Claude 3 Sonnet). This doesn't mean
  base models are wrong — it means IT models are valid targets. But IT models require
  matching the training data format to the inference format.
